What is promised, and what is not

The unit of promise is a site × operation record, not a website. An operation that has not been evaluated inherits nothing from another operation on the same site.

The fixture site is not on this list on purpose. It is our own evaluation environment — built by us, for mutation and safety testing — and it is not a supported website. Promising operations on a site we wrote ourselves would be setting our own exam. The fixture operations exercised in this build (GS-1 POST-only search, GS-2 client-side pagination, GS-3 overlay dismissal) are gate evidence and appear in no success-rate figure.

Breadth is handled by the experimental tier rather than by counting declared sites: any public, policy-clean, read-only site may be attempted, and where a result cannot be verified the system abstains and says where it stopped. That is a chosen trade-off — depth on locator memory and the safety suite over breadth of declared sites.

Known limitations, concretely

  • This build is M1. There is no model in the loop and no verifier, so every run that produces a value ends unverified. Nothing here should be read as a correctness claim.
  • verified will not mean "true". It will mean the claim is consistent with a preserved artifact and passes deterministic type, unit, date and entity checks. A value that is correct in form but taken from the wrong row, entity or period can still pass; label→value structural binding narrows this without closing it.
  • Necessary UI actions are declared, not proven. The harness verifies that a case declared an action as required and the trace shows it happened. It does not prove the action was impossible to bypass. Only the fixture's GS-1/GS-2/GS-3 are shortcut-proof by construction.
  • Injection is mitigated structurally, not prevented. Goal, postcondition and policy live outside model-mutable state, the executor accepts a fixed action allow-list, and navigation is checked against an origin policy. Page content still necessarily influences the next action.
  • DNS rebinding is not solved. Addresses are checked at resolve time and the connection is not pinned to them.

Site policy

books.toscrape.com was chosen because its automation policy is unambiguous — it is a public sandbox built for automation practice and serves no robots.txt at all. arXiv was dropped because its robots policy forbids the operations we needed: Crawl-delay: 15, Disallow: /search, /find, /form, /api, and a stated policy that indiscriminate automated downloads are not permitted. Neither choice is a research narrative.

Per-origin pacing is our own voluntary limit, not compliance with a robots directive. Wikipedia's Crawl-delay: 5 applies to a named bot, not to User-agent: *, so no crawl-delay is imposed on us there. SEC EDGAR is self-limited to 1 request/second against their published cap of 10, with a declared contact User-Agent — which their server requires, not merely requests.
